    Frequently Asked Questions About Faucet Repair in Parkersburg

    The cost of faucet repair in Parkersburg can range from $120 to $350, depending on the type of faucet, the specific parts needed, and the complexity of the repair. A simple washer replacement will be on the lower end, while a cartridge replacement in a high-end faucet might be more. We provide a clear estimate before any work begins.

    Water hardness in West Virginia ranges from moderate to hard in many areas, and mineral scale is a common issue for water heaters and fixtures. Hard water contains high levels of minerals like calcium and magnesium. Over time, these minerals can build up inside your faucets, corroding internal parts, clogging aerators, and stiffening moving components. This often leads to leaks, reduced water pressure, and a shorter faucet lifespan, making regular maintenance or prompt repair important.

    Most repairs take 30 to 60 minutes. If a special part must be ordered, we may need a second short visit.

    While some minor leaks, like a simple washer replacement on a compression faucet, can be a DIY project, most modern faucets involve specific tools and intricate parts like cartridges or ceramic discs. Incorrect repairs can lead to further damage, more significant leaks, or voided warranties. It's usually best to call a professional for a proper and lasting fix.

    For a standard faucet repair, where only internal components are replaced and the fixture itself remains, no plumbing permits are usually required in Parkersburg. However, if the repair involves altering the existing plumbing lines or installing a new type of fixture that requires new connections, a permit might be necessary. We will let you know if any permits are needed for your specific situation.

    Faucet repair involves fixing the existing fixture by replacing specific worn or broken internal components like washers, O-rings, or cartridges. Faucet replacement means removing the old faucet entirely and installing a brand new one. We recommend replacement when a faucet is severely damaged, very old, or if you simply want an upgrade.

    The most common signs include a persistent drip or leak, difficulty turning the handles, inconsistent water temperature, reduced water pressure from the faucet, or strange noises when operating it. Any of these could indicate internal component wear or damage requiring attention.

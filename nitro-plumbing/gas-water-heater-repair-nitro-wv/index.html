    Pilot Light Repair in Nitro

    A pilot that will not light or will not stay lit is the most common gas water heater call. Causes include a worn thermocouple, a clogged pilot orifice, a bad piezo igniter or a tripped thermal cutoff. We test the thermocouple output with a meter and service or replace the pilot assembly. Most pilot repairs are finished in about an hour.

    Emergency Gas Water Heater Repair in Nitro

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Nitro

    Rural properties often run water heaters on propane instead of natural gas. Propane units use different orifices and regulator pressures, and incorrect conversions cause sooting and poor performance. We confirm the correct parts are installed and repair pilots, valves and burners. We also check tank regulators for correct pressure.

    Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Nitro

    Power vent heaters use a blower to push exhaust out through PVC pipe. Failures often involve the blower motor, pressure switch, hot surface igniter or condensate in the vent. We read the error codes, test each part and repair the failure. Correct vent slope and termination are verified before we leave.

    Tank Leaks and Pressure Problems in Nitro

    Even gas water heaters can develop tank leaks, often due to corrosion from hard water or high water pressure. Testing well water every year or so is a good habit in West Virginia, especially after flooding or nearby ground disturbance. While some leaks might require a replacement, minor leaks or pressure relief valve issues can often be repaired.     Burner Assembly and Venting System Repair in Nitro

    A worn burner assembly can result in insufficient heating or unusual noises. We closely inspect the burner for blockages, corrosion or wear. We also check the venting system for proper draft and obstructions, which is essential for safe operation and preventing carbon monoxide buildup in your Nitro home. Proper venting is a critical safety consideration.

    Gas Control Valve Replacement in Nitro, WV

    The gas control valve controls temperature and shuts off gas if the pilot goes out. Newer valves from Honeywell and White-Rodgers use status lights that blink codes when something fails. If the valve itself has failed, we replace it with an exact match or listed universal model. We leak test and confirm temperature control afterward.

    Pipe Bursting Sewer Line Replacement in Fairmont

    Pipe bursting is a trenchless method used for full sewer line replacement. It involves pulling a new pipe through the old one while fracturing the existing pipe. This creates a seamless, durable replacement, often using high-density polyethylene pipe, which is highly resistant to corrosion and root intrusion, common problems in older Fairmont homes. This method is ideal when the existing pipe is severely damaged or collapsed.

    Root Intrusion Repair in Fairmont, WV

    Tree and shrub roots go after moisture in sewer joints. Once inside, they grow into dense masses that cause repeat backups. We cut roots with a mechanical cutter or hydro jet, then line the pipe to seal the joints. That breaks the cycle of cleaning the line every few months.

    Sewer Line Video Inspection in Fairmont

    Before any trenchless repair, we perform a thorough video inspection of your sewer line. This involves inserting a high-resolution camera into the pipe to accurately diagnose the problem's exact location and nature. This crucial step makes sure we choose the most appropriate and effective trenchless method for your Fairmont property, avoiding guesswork.

    Orangeburg and Clay Pipe Repair in Fairmont

    Some older homes still have Orangeburg fiber pipe or vitrified clay sewer laterals. Orangeburg flattens and blisters over time, while clay cracks and separates at joints. Pipe bursting is often the best option for Orangeburg, and lining can work well for clay that still holds its shape. We determine the material on camera before recommending a method.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Lining in Fairmont

    Cured-in-place lining creates a new pipe inside the old one. We clean the line, then pull or invert a resin-saturated liner into place and cure it with hot water, steam or UV light. The result is a seamless, jointless pipe that roots cannot penetrate. Lining works well for cracked or root-intruded lines that still hold their form.

    Pipe Bursting in Fairmont, WV

    Pipe bursting replaces a badly damaged line by pulling a bursting head through the old pipe. The head breaks the old pipe outward while new HDPE pipe follows behind it. Bursting is best for collapsed or severely offset lines that cannot be lined. It typically requires an entry pit and an exit pit.

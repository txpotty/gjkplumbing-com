    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Weirton

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper costs more, and yard service lines typically add $1,500 to $4,000.

    We primarily use quality, trusted brands for PEX such as Uponor (formerly Wirsbo), Viega, and Zurn, known for their durability and performance. For copper, we use standard Type L or Type M copper tubing from reputable manufacturers, providing long-lasting reliability.

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water break down the plastic from the inside, making it brittle. Fittings and hot water lines often fail first.

    Common signs include frequent leaks, discolored water, low water pressure, or visible grey or blue plastic pipes often marked 'PB2110'. If your home was built between 1978 and 1995 and has these pipes, replacement is strongly advised.

    Many West Virginia homes rely on private wells or springs, and that water can carry iron, manganese or sulfur that stains fixtures and laundry. While hard water doesn't directly cause polybutylene to fail like chlorine does, it can contribute to scale buildup within the pipes. This buildup can reduce water pressure and potentially add to problems if the pipe material is already compromised.

    Both PEX and copper are excellent choices. PEX is flexible, less expensive, more freeze-resistant, and handles ground movement better than rigid pipe. Copper offers superior rigidity, a long lifespan, and a strong track record. The 'better' choice depends on your budget, preferences, and home's specific needs.

    A typical full home polybutylene repipe in Weirton, WV can take anywhere from 3 to 7 days, depending on the size and complexity of your home. We work efficiently to limit disruption and will provide a clear timeline during your initial consultation.

    We commonly use Uponor PEX-A and Type L copper with Viega ProPress or soldered fittings. We recommend materials based on your home and water conditions.

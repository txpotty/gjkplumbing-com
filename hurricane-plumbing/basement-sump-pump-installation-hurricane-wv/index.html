    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Hurricane

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab usually falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    A standard basement sump pump installation usually takes 4 to 8 hours. If a new sump pit needs to be excavated, or there are unexpected challenges with plumbing or electrical access, the job might stretch to a full day. Our team works efficiently to limit disruption to your home.

    Watch for a pump that runs constantly, cycles on and off rapidly, makes grinding noises or never seems to turn on. Rust stains, a stuck float and water sitting above the switch level are also red flags.

    Manufactured homes are common in rural West Virginia, and their plumbing often needs extra attention to skirting, insulation and freeze protection. Homes built on slabs without basements seldom need one. Homes that do have basements or below-grade rooms can take on water quickly during heavy storms, especially where the soil sheds rain instead of absorbing it.

    Swapping a pump in an existing pit typically takes two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and concrete patching usually takes most of a day.

    We regularly install Zoeller, Liberty Pumps and Wayne because they offer cast iron housings, reliable switches and good parts support. We will match the brand and model to your pit size and budget.

    GJK Plumbing provides a warranty on our labor for basement sump pump installations, typically for one year. The sump pump itself comes with a manufacturer's warranty, which can range from 1 to 5 years depending on the brand and model. We will provide you all warranty details at the time of installation.

    Usually, standard home insurance policies do not cover a new sump pump installation or repairs unless the damage was caused by a sudden, accidental event like a burst pipe, rather than gradual wear and tear or groundwater seepage. Some policies offer separate water backup or sump pump failure endorsements for an additional premium. It is best to check with your insurance provider.

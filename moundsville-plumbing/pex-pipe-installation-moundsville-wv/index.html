    Frequently Asked Questions About PEX Pipe Installation in Moundsville

    A whole-home PEX repipe typically costs $4,500 to $12,000 depending on home size, bathroom count and access. Smaller jobs like a single bathroom remodel rough-in usually run $800 to $2,500.

    PEX piping is designed to be highly durable and has an estimated lifespan of 50 years or more. Its resistance to corrosion, scaling and freeze damage adds to its long-term reliability, making it a sound investment for your Moundsville home.

    PEX offers several advantages over copper, including lower material and installation costs, greater flexibility and resistance to corrosion and freezing. Unlike copper, PEX does not form pinhole leaks over time due to water chemistry, making it a more durable choice for many Moundsville homes. It also reduces water hammer noise.

    PEX can be used for outdoor applications like irrigation lines or outdoor spigots, but it needs to be protected from direct UV light, which can break down the material over time. When used outdoors, PEX should always be buried or otherwise shielded.

    PEX resists scale and corrosion, flexes with temperature changes and has fewer joints. Copper is very durable but prone to pinhole leaks in some water conditions and costs more to install.

    GJK Plumbing stands behind our PEX pipe installations with a full warranty on both labor and materials. Specific warranty details will be provided with your estimate, but you can be sure your new PEX system is installed correctly and will perform reliably for years to come in your Moundsville home.

    A full home PEX repipe for an average-sized home in Moundsville typically takes 3 to 5 days. Smaller projects or single-line replacements can often be completed in a single day. We aim to minimize disruption and communicate timelines clearly.

    Signs you might need PEX repiping include persistently low water pressure, discolored or rusty water, frequent leaks, or old galvanized steel or polybutylene pipes. These issues are common in older Moundsville homes and point to a failing plumbing system.
